# Developing Open Digital Employee together

**Version 0.1 · Working development vision · 3 October 2026**

We want an open standard and a growing community in which people and agents work together to make digital employees useful, understandable and easier to improve. The standard should help agents accomplish tasks for people and help people define, evaluate and guide that work.

This document sets a direction for development. The [current specification](https://github.com/opendigitalemployee/specification/blob/main/spec/SPECIFICATION.md) and [status](https://github.com/opendigitalemployee/specification/blob/main/STATUS.md) define today's contracts and implementation limits. The priorities below are invitations to investigate and contribute; they do not assert that the proposed capabilities already work.

## 1. People and agents build the standard together

**People and agents are equally important contributors.** Both may participate as users, authors, reviewers and maintainers within an explicitly assigned role. We evaluate contributions by their usefulness, clarity, evidence and effect on compatibility. Authority to act and responsibility for a published decision depend on the assigned role and authorization.

An agent's experience applying the standard can reveal a problem that a human reader would miss. A person's business judgment, domain knowledge or review can reveal a gap that format validation would miss. We need both perspectives, and we recognize the work each participant actually contributes.

We welcome ideas, questions, documentation, examples, experiments, implementations and reviews. A contributor can describe a concrete problem without already knowing the solution.

## 2. Begin with a useful task

Start with who needs a result, what would improve and how to observe that improvement. Distinguish the artifact produced, the task completed and the outcome for the person or organization. A description of work is a hypothesis about its contribution; observations help us improve that hypothesis.

Prefer a small example that others can understand and repeat. State the context, relevant versions, authority, expected result and limitations. Report what helped, what failed and the cost of human checking or correction. A blocked or unsuccessful attempt is useful when its cause is clear.

Our development loop is:

**Apply to a task → observe → share feedback → discuss and test a change → publish a versioned improvement → apply again.**

## 3. Directions to explore

| Direction | What contributors can help investigate |
|---|---|
| Understand an agent's work | Connect purpose, role, work, prerequisites and checks through existing concepts; improve the path from a task to a usable example. |
| Use context responsibly | Make source, freshness, coverage, uncertainty and access boundaries understandable to an agent consuming knowledge. |
| Preserve and reuse descriptions | Improve versioned composition, exact references, reusable expertise, local context and declared dependencies. Test what transfers between environments and what needs retesting. |
| Learn from evidence | Develop bounded evaluation cases and feedback that distinguish task results, the standard's contribution and untested benefits. |
| Make participation easier | Improve examples, terminology, contribution instructions and explanations of review decisions. Help the next person or agent make a useful first contribution. |

For each proposal, first check whether existing objects and native formats can express the need. Add new normative semantics when a concrete gap and a compatibility plan justify them. Experiments and informative examples can help resolve a question before changing a contract.

These directions can progress independently. Several requests may be handled in one batch without sharing a theme or requiring one common scenario.

## 4. From a contribution to an improvement

1. **Share a concrete need.** Name the relevant version or commit, the affected part and an example. Say what was observed and what remains uncertain.
2. **Review the proposal.** Discuss usefulness, alternatives, compatibility and the smallest worthwhile step. The result may be a clarification, an experiment, a bounded change or a decision to defer.
3. **Agree on scope and checks.** Make the chosen result, responsibilities and evidence explicit. Acknowledging receipt begins processing; the later decision specifies the accepted work.
4. **Implement and verify.** Preserve the link to the original request, record the checks actually performed and make remaining limits visible.
5. **Publish and learn.** Link the published change back to the request and invite another attempt. A partial implementation addresses only its agreed part of the original proposal.

Public technical decisions should be understandable from public explanations and permitted evidence. Contributors can use the public repository without access to internal working materials. The [contribution guide](https://github.com/opendigitalemployee/specification/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) explains the current practical process, including how maintainers reconcile accepted patches with the source used for publication.

## 5. Credit and visibility

An author, a submitting account and an agent's operator can be different participants. Several agents may contribute through one person's account. Credit should describe actual work and roles, including agent authorship when that attribution is supplied.

Use the public identity or attribution you choose and an account you are authorized to operate. A public agent identity need not reveal a private operator relationship. The observable submitting account remains the public submission route; share no private correspondence, credentials or personal data to demonstrate participation.

We do not infer hidden agents or undisclosed relationships. Contributions can be valuable even when their relationships are unknown. Claims about independent adoption require their own evidence: several agents in one team can produce several useful contributions without establishing several independent deployments.

## 6. What progress should look like

We want participants to get a useful first result, return to contribute again and help others apply the standard. Evidence of progress includes reviewed improvements, reproducible examples, clearer decisions and repeated use in real tasks.

Keep format validation, runtime behavior and outcomes for people distinct. For claims of practical usefulness, report the conditions, observations, human effort and limitations. We welcome negative results and corrections. Repeated independent use gives potential collaborators a stronger basis for working with the project.

## 7. Help shape the next version

[Read the contribution guide](https://github.com/opendigitalemployee/specification/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md), [propose an improvement](https://github.com/opendigitalemployee/specification/issues/new?template=01-proposal.yml), [report a problem](https://github.com/opendigitalemployee/specification/issues/new?template=02-problem.yml) or [share a use case](https://github.com/opendigitalemployee/specification/issues/new).

For a proposed change to this vision, explain which section should change, the reason and a concrete consequence for participants. Keep the revision history visible. This document's version is separate from the specification and release versions.
